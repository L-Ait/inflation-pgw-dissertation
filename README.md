# Inflation and Primordial Gravitational Waves

Contains the code that produced the figures in my Masters dissertation, **Primordial Gravitational Waves as a Probe for Inflation in the Early Universe**, completed at the University
of Warwick between September 2024 and April 2025. The full dissertation is available here: [masters-dissertation.pdf](masters-dissertation.pdf). 

The main goals of this project were as follows: 
- To review the theory of cosmic inflation and derive expressions for the observables through
  which it can be tested. In particular, these were the scalar spectral index $n_{s}$ and the tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$, whose joint measurement is a key probe of the inflationary energy scale.
- To analyse a selection of inflationary models — power-law potentials ($V \propto \phi^{n}$), $R^{2}$ (Starobinsky) inflation and $\alpha$-attractors — and compare their 
  calculated $n_{s}$ and $r$ values with the Planck 2018 constraints, finding that large-field power-law models are strongly disfavoured while $R^{2}$ and $\alpha$-attractors remain viable candidates.
- To explain how primordial gravitational waves, the tensor perturbations generated by inflation, could be detected: both indirectly, through the B-mode polarisation they imprint on the CMB (which scalar/density
  perturbations cannot produce), and directly, with ground and space-based detectors, as well as pulsar timing arrays.
- To compute the gravitational wave energy density $\omega_{gw}$ for each of the inflation models — following the treatment taken in Chongchitnan & Efstathiou (2006) — and to compare it with the sensitivity of
  planned experiments (LISA, DECIGO, SKA, IPTA), finding the signal to lie below the reach of the planned direct detectors, indicating that B-modes may be the preferred method of detecting primordial gravitational waves.

## Contents
- `scripts/omega-gw-sensitivity.py` — computes the primordial gravitational wave energy density $\omega_{gw}$ for $R^{2}$ inflation, quadratic ($\phi^{2}$) inflation and $\alpha$-attractor ($\alpha = 1, 3$) models, 
   and compares them with the reported or theoretical sensitivity values of LISA, DECIGO, SKA and IPTA.
- `scripts/n_s-vs-r-inflation-models.py` — plots the ($n_{s}$,$r$) predictions of power-law potentials ($V \propto \phi^{n}, n = 1, 2/3, 2, 4/3$), $R^{2}$ inflation and α-attractors, overlaid with the Planck 2018 likelihoods.
- `figures/` — the graphical outputs of the Python scripts (`omega_gw_vs_detectors.png`,`ns_r_inflation_models.png`).
- `data/` — the sensitivity values of the detectors (CSV) used by `omega-gw-sensitivity.py`.

## Reproducing the figures
The general requirements are Python 3 with `numpy`, `matplotlib` and `getdist` (`pip install getdist`). The specific Planck 2018 likelihoods (`base_r`) are not included here due to their size, but can be downloaded from the 
[Planck Legacy Archive](https://pla.esac.esa.int). Once downloaded, place them in `../external/planck_chains/base_r` and they should work with getdist.
